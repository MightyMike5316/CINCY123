A little window education

How a vintage double-hung window actually works

Each sash hangs from two cotton cords that run over pulleys in the frame and down to cast-iron weights hidden in a pocket behind the trim. The weights balance the sash, which is why a healthy old window glides with one finger and stays put wherever you leave it.

When a cord breaks, the weight drops to the bottom of the pocket and the sash loses its balance — it slams shut or has to be propped open. Paint buildup glues the sash to its stops. Glazing putty dries and falls out, and the glass works loose.

None of that is a reason to replace the window. All of it is routine for Mark. See each repair in detail.

West Side houses

The windows in Cincinnati's older neighborhoods

Westwood, Price Hill, Cheviot, Covedale, Sayler Park and the older parts of Delhi and Green Township are full of houses built between the 1880s and the 1950s — brick foursquares, Tudors, Cape Cods and bungalows with their original wood windows still in place. Those windows were made from dense old-growth lumber, with glass and hardware meant to be serviced rather than thrown away.

Mark has worked on all of them. If your home is one of these, there's a very good chance your windows can be made to work like new for a fraction of the cost of replacement. Give him a call and find out.
