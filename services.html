Double-hung window repair: sash cords, weights & stuck sashes

A double-hung window has two sashes that slide past each other, each balanced by iron weights on cords that run over pulleys. When a cord breaks, the sash slams shut or won't stay up. When paint builds up, it won't move at all.

Common problems

  • Sash won't stay open, or falls shut on its own
  • Frayed, broken or missing sash cord; weight loose in the pocket
  • Sash painted or swollen shut
  • Rattling, drafty sashes that don't meet tightly at the lock
  • Missing or broken sash lock, lifts or pulleys

What Mark does

  • Opens the weight pocket, retrieves the weights and re-ropes with proper sash cord
  • Rebalances so both sashes glide and hold anywhere in the frame
  • Frees painted-shut sashes and eases binding without damaging the trim
  • Replaces worn pulleys, locks and lifts with period-appropriate hardware

Glazing repair & repainting

Glazing putty is the beveled seal that holds each pane in its wood sash. After decades it dries, cracks and falls out. Water gets behind the glass, the wood starts to rot and the pane loosens and rattles.

Common problems

  • Cracked, crumbling or missing putty
  • Loose or rattling panes; a cracked or broken light
  • Peeling paint and soft wood along the bottom rail
  • Condensation and drafts around the glass

What Mark does

  • Removes failed glazing and old points; cleans the rabbet
  • Resets the original pane (or fits new glass if it's broken) in a fresh bed of putty
  • Tools a clean new putty bevel, then primes and paints for a lasting seal
  • Repairs minor rot and resets loose joints while the sash is apart

Glazing is often done together with re-roping so the window is finished in one visit.

Hinges & operators

Casement window repair

Casement windows swing out on side hinges, usually driven by a crank operator. Over time hinges wear, operators strip and the sash sags so it no longer pulls tight — which means drafts and water.

Common problems

  • Crank grinds, slips or spins without moving the sash
  • Sash sags, drags on the sill or won't latch
  • Won't close tight; air and water come in at the edge
  • Worn or broken hinges, latches and stays

What Mark does

  • Repairs or replaces operators, hinges, latches and stays
  • Re-hangs and adjusts the sash so it closes square and seals
  • Eases binding edges and renews weather-stripping where needed
  • Re-glazes and repaints the sash while it's accessible (see glazing)
Protect the originals

Storm panel repair

Storm windows take the weather so your original windows don't have to. A storm that's loose, cracked or no longer fits the opening isn't protecting anything — but it rarely needs to be replaced.

Common problems

  • Broken or missing glass; loose panes in the frame
  • Bent, split or rotted frames; failed corners
  • Panels that no longer fit, won't latch or whistle in the wind
  • Missing hangers, clips or screens

What Mark does

  • Repairs wood and aluminum storm frames and re-squares them
  • Replaces broken glass and re-glazes or re-splines panels
  • Refits panels to the opening and replaces hangers and hardware
  • Advises honestly when a storm panel is beyond repair

Common questions

Old window repair FAQ

Is it really cheaper to repair old windows than replace them?

Almost always. Replacing a single window with a decent new unit plus installation typically runs well into four figures per opening, while re-roping, re-glazing and weather-tightening an original window costs a fraction of that and lasts for decades. Call Mark with the number of windows and what they're doing and he'll give you a straight estimate.

My window has been painted shut for years. Can it be freed without damage?

Yes. Painted-shut sashes are one of the most common calls Mark gets. The paint seal is cut, the sash is freed and planed or waxed where it binds, and the cords and weights are checked so it actually stays open once it moves again.

What is the rope inside my window for, and why did it break?

That's the sash cord. It runs over a pulley at the top of the frame to a cast-iron weight hidden in the wall, which balances the sash so it stays wherever you put it. Cotton cords dry out and fray after decades. Mark replaces them with proper waxed sash cord (or chain where appropriate) and re-hangs the weights.

Can you replace a cracked pane in an old wood window?

Yes. A single broken pane is reset with new glass, bedded and glazed, then primed and painted. You don't need to replace the whole sash — let alone the whole window — for one cracked light.

Does Mark repair vinyl or aluminum replacement windows?

No. Mark specializes in the original wood double-hung and casement windows, and the storm panels that protect them, found in Cincinnati homes built roughly from the 1880s through the 1950s. If your windows are newer replacement units, he'll tell you so on the phone and save you a trip.
